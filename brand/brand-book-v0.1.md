# R&R Finest Auto Glass Brand Book v0.1

Status: ACCEPTED

## 1. Purpose

This brand book defines the initial identity system for R&R Finest Auto Glass across product, operations, customer, auction, insurance, and future marketing experiences.

The goal is to create one recognizable R&R product family while allowing each channel to adapt to its audience.

This document defines brand direction. It does not yet define the complete UI component system.

---

## 2. Brand Positioning

R&R Finest Auto Glass should present as:

- reliable;
- capable;
- responsive;
- practical;
- modern without feeling over-designed;
- transparent about process;
- professional enough for insurance and auction partners;
- approachable enough for direct customers.

The brand should communicate:

> We know the work, we keep the job moving, and we make the process easy to understand.

---

## 3. Brand Promise

R&R helps customers and partners move from damaged glass to completed service with clear communication, dependable execution, and minimal friction.

Operationally, the product experience should reinforce:

- speed;
- clarity;
- confidence;
- accountability;
- visibility into what happens next.

---

## 4. Brand Personality

Primary traits:

### Dependable
The brand should feel steady and trustworthy.

### Direct
Use plain language. Avoid unnecessary jargon.

### Capable
The system should feel competent and operationally sharp.

### Responsive
Users should feel that work is moving, not disappearing into a black box.

### Human
R&R should feel like a real service business run by people, not an impersonal enterprise portal.

---

## 5. Brand Voice

### Tone

Preferred tone:
- clear;
- concise;
- confident;
- respectful;
- practical.

Avoid:
- inflated claims;
- vague corporate language;
- overly playful copy in operational or insurance contexts;
- technical terminology when a simpler phrase works;
- anxiety-inducing language for recoverable issues.

### Example language

Preferred:

`Your quote is ready.`

`We are verifying the correct glass for your vehicle.`

`Installation is scheduled for Friday.`

`This job needs your attention.`

Avoid:

`Your request has advanced into downstream procurement orchestration.`

`A critical exception has occurred!`

---

## 7. Visual Direction

The visual system should feel:

- dark, modern, and operational for internal tools;
- clean and lighter for direct customer experiences where appropriate;
- consistent through shared typography, spacing, shapes, and brand accents;
- information-dense without becoming visually noisy.

The visual language should prioritize function over decoration.

---

## 8. Color Direction

v0.1 brand direction:

### Primary Dark
Use for internal operational surfaces and strong brand framing.

Suggested family:
- deep navy / near-black

### Primary Accent
Use for active states, primary actions, links, and selected navigation.

Suggested family:
- cool blue / indigo

### Positive
Use for completed, confirmed, available, approved states.

Suggested family:
- green / teal

### Attention
Use for review, waiting, warning, or action-needed states.

Suggested family:
- amber

### Critical
Use for failed, blocked, cancelled, or severe exception states.

Suggested family:
- red / rose

### Neutral
Use for borders, metadata, secondary text, inactive controls.

Suggested family:
- slate / cool gray

Exact tokens belong in the Design Guide, not this document.

---

## 9. Typography Direction

Typography should prioritize legibility, speed of scanning, and broad device support.

Preferred characteristics:
- sans serif;
- strong readability at small operational sizes;
- clear distinction between headings, labels, and body text;
- no decorative display type in core application workflows.

System-safe or broadly available modern sans-serif families are preferred unless brand typography is later formally selected.

---

## 10. Shape and Surface Language

Preferred:
- moderate corner radius;
- restrained shadows;
- bordered cards and panels;
- compact status badges;
- clear separation between primary and secondary actions;
- consistent spacing rhythm.

Avoid:
- excessive gradients;
- glassmorphism as a dominant UI pattern;
- heavy drop shadows;
- novelty shapes;
- decorative animation that competes with task completion.

---

## 11. Logo / Mark Direction

Current product prototypes use a simple `R&R` mark.

Until a final logo system is approved:

- use the R&R wordmark/initial mark consistently;
- do not stretch or distort it;
- maintain clear space around it;
- avoid multiple competing logo treatments inside the product;
- keep logo use secondary to task completion in operational screens.

A future formal logo package may supersede this section.

---

## 12. Imagery Direction

If imagery is used:

Preferred:
- real vehicles;
- technicians at work;
- windshield/glass installation;
- service environments;
- authentic R&R operations.

Avoid:
- generic corporate stock photography;
- unrelated luxury-car imagery that misrepresents the service;
- overly staged technician imagery;
- visual clutter in operational screens.

Internal staff tools should generally avoid unnecessary imagery.

---

## 13. Iconography Direction

Icons should:
- reinforce labels, not replace them where meaning may be ambiguous;
- use a consistent stroke/fill style;
- remain readable at small sizes;
- prioritize familiar operational symbols.

Examples:
- vehicle;
- quote/document;
- calendar;
- order/package;
- warning;
- check/completed;
- phone/message;
- history/activity.

---

## 14. Status Semantics

Brand color must not be the only carrier of status meaning.

Recommended semantic categories:

- Neutral
- In progress
- Completed
- Attention required
- Blocked / failed
- Cancelled

The exact mapping of workflow states into these semantic categories belongs in the Design Guide.

---

## 15. Brand Consistency Across Channels

The four experiences may differ in density and vocabulary, but should remain visibly related.

Shared elements should include:
- typography family;
- accent color family;
- button language;
- radius/shape language;
- status semantics;
- spacing rhythm;
- R&R mark usage;
- interaction feedback.

Channel distinction should come from:
- information hierarchy;
- terminology;
- density;
- workflow emphasis;
- permissions;
- action priority.

Not from creating four unrelated visual brands.

---

## 16. Brand Do / Don't

### Do

- make the next action obvious;
- use plain English;
- show users where they are in the process;
- reinforce confidence through clarity;
- use consistent status language;
- keep operational interfaces compact and useful;
- adapt vocabulary to the persona.

### Don't

- expose internal complexity unnecessarily;
- use marketing language inside operational workflows;
- overwhelm direct customers with supplier or margin data;
- use color without text/status context;
- create different visual identities for each channel;
- sacrifice legibility for visual novelty.

---

## 17. Accessibility Brand Principle

Accessibility is part of brand quality.

The R&R brand should not rely on:
- tiny text;
- low contrast;
- color-only signaling;
- hover-only interactions;
- overly dense mobile layouts.

A reliable service brand should also feel reliable to use.

## 19. Brand Acceptance Criteria

The brand direction is acceptable when:

- R&R has a clear personality and voice;
- channel-specific language is defined;
- visual direction is coherent across all personas;
- no channel becomes a separate visual brand;
- accessibility is treated as part of brand quality;
- the Design Guide can derive concrete tokens/components from this document;
- open brand questions remain explicit rather than silently assumed.

## 22. Provisional Brand Decisions Pending Client Validation

These decisions are working brand assumptions for product implementation. They should be reviewed with the client before being treated as final brand standards.

### BR-PD-001 — Logo
**Working decision:** Treat the current simple `R&R` mark as the official **v0.1 product mark**, while leaving room for a future professional logo system.

**Rationale:** It gives the product a consistent identity now without blocking delivery on a full identity redesign.

### BR-PD-002 — Primary accent
**Working decision:** Keep **blue/indigo** as the primary brand accent.

**Rationale:** It works well across operational, customer, auction, and insurance contexts and supports the reliable/modern positioning already accepted.

### BR-PD-003 — Customer visual mode
**Working decision:** Direct Customer experiences use a **predominantly light visual system**; R&R internal operational tools remain predominantly dark.

**Rationale:** Light customer surfaces feel simpler and more familiar for transactional consumer flows, while dark internal surfaces support the dense operational workspace already prototyped. Shared tokens keep both in the same family.

### BR-PD-004 — Photography
**Working decision:** Use authentic R&R photography selectively on public/marketing/customer entry surfaces, but keep transactional workflow screens primarily interface-driven.

**Rationale:** Real photography can humanize and establish trust, while operational screens should prioritize task completion.

### BR-PD-005 — Tagline
**Working decision:** Do **not** force a public tagline in v0.1.

**Rationale:** The current product has stronger need for clear service communication than a slogan. A tagline can be developed later with the client if it adds real brand value.
